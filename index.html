Philosophy

Interaction is felt before it's understood.

The timing of a fade. The resistance in a swipe. The breath-like pause before a view settles. These are design decisions most interfaces leave to defaults — and they compound into a feeling people can't name but absolutely register.

I study how things move in the physical world — leaves decelerating before they land, the spring-back of a branch — and I bring that back to the screen. When digital motion borrows from nature, people feel it.

Motion

Easing curves drawn from physics, not convention. Transitions that carry meaning rather than fill time.

Flow

User journeys that follow the logic of the body — not the logic of the database behind them.

Restraint

Every frame earns its place or gets cut. The pause is part of the motion — silence is a design decision too.

Presence

Interactions designed to end — putting you back in your body, outdoors, or with the people around you.

How I Work

Three-phase process

01  —  Research

Start in the world

Field observation, contextual interviews, bodystorming. I understand what a person feels before they open the app — before any wireframe exists.

02  —  Structure

Logic before visuals

Information architecture, user flows, and low-fidelity wireframes drawn on paper first. The interaction logic is nailed before Figma opens.

03  —  Motion

Prototype to test

High-fidelity prototypes with full motion specs — easing curves, duration ratios, state transitions — built to test, not to present.
