Background

I came to design through noticing what wasn't working.

Most apps I used to connect with nature, my body, and my neighbors felt like productivity software — forms to fill, streaks to maintain, notifications that pulled me away from the thing I was trying to pay attention to. I wanted to learn how to design differently.

Information Systems gave me the structural foundation: how data is organized, how systems are specified, how decisions made in architecture surface as experience in the interface. Motion design gave me the vocabulary for the felt layer — the 180 milliseconds that either build trust or quietly undermine it.

My design practice is rooted in the physical world. I study how leaves decelerate before they settle. How people orient in unfamiliar outdoor spaces. These observations shape every transition, every pause, every affordance I design.

IS 436 Learning Outcomes

What this course added to the practice

📐
Wireframing

IS 436 enforced structure I already believed in — architecture committed to PDF before Figma opens. The constraint improved the thinking every time.

🔄
SDLC

A design process that doesn't account for handoff and iteration isn't a process — it's a presentation. SDLC gave me respect for the full arc.

⚙️
System Design

Interaction decisions have downstream consequences on databases and APIs. Understanding the system makes my design decisions more honest.

♿
Accessibility

WCAG is a discipline of care. Motion that respects vestibular disorders. Contrast ratios that hold in direct sunlight. Now part of my default thinking.

🐳
Docker & DevOps

Knowing how software deploys changes how I design for degraded states — slow connections, failed loads, offline conditions.

🌿
Git & GitHub

Version control taught me that design is iterative and decisions compound. Public thinking gets sharper.
